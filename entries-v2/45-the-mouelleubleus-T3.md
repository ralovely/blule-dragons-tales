There is blue dust on my notebook again. A young dragon rubbed against my bag this morning, and now everything in it has a faint blue cast. My pencils. My spare shirt. The sandwich I was saving for later.

The Manaïari call them the Mouelleubleus. I asked Manami to translate. She said it means, roughly, "the soft blue ones," then paused and admitted it also sounds like the noise a cat makes when it is unimpressed. She suspects a child coined it. I suspect she is right.

They are dragons who have lived so long near blue stone that the pigment has seeped into their scales. The deeper the blue, the older the dragon, the longer it has slept and eaten and breathed in the stone's company. Among them, colour is biography. A pale blue is young, or a newcomer. A deep, almost black blue has been here since before anyone can remember. The young ones, naturally, cheat. They roll in blue dust to look older and more distinguished. It fools no one. The dust sits on top of the scales instead of inside them, and rubs off on everything. It is the dragon equivalent of a false moustache, and about as convincing.
