There is a species here that is, underwater, astonishingly graceful. Sleek and fluid, almost seal-like. Out of the water, its fur absorbs roughly four times its weight and it emerges looking like a soaked sheep that has been struck by lightning. The transformation is so complete I did not recognise the first one I saw climbing out.

The drying process involves a full-body shake of such violence that everything within ten feet gets drenched. River and I learnt this the hard way. Then we learnt it the hard way again, because neither of us had moved far enough. Cendre, who had been standing behind a tree the entire time, remained dry and said nothing. He is his father's son. The dragon looked apologetic for exactly half a second before shaking a third time.

One dragon will not leave the water if anyone is watching. It sits in the shallows, chin just above the surface, scanning the bank until every last witness has gone. It once waited three hours. The Manaïari have learnt to walk away with great ceremony and then circle back to peek from behind trees. The dragon knows. It waits longer.
