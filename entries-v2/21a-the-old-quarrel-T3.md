There is an old dragon here I have sketched a dozen times without ever drawing his left side. Today he rolled over in the sun and I saw why I had been avoiding it. Four scars, long and parallel, from shoulder to hip, old enough to have gone pale and smooth. Too clean for rockfall. Nothing like the hunters' work I spent twenty years cataloguing. I measured the spread of the lines against my hand, and then against his own forefoot. Claws made those, and no small ones.

I asked Manami. She said, "He went south once." I waited for the rest. There was no rest.

At the racks that evening, the knot-keeper found me the year. I did not tell him what I was looking for; I suspect the question has been asked before. It is the only ugly knot I have seen there. Two cords forced through each other and pulled until they fused, singed brown at one end. It has never been untied. He let me hold it for a moment, then put it back among the thousands of ordinary years, where you would never find it unless someone showed you.

The old dragon was asleep in the square when I walked home, chin on his claws, children stepping over his tail. He sleeps on his scarred side, I notice. Perhaps it no longer troubles him. Perhaps he prefers to keep it against the ground.
