On slow mornings, when the mist is thick and nothing much is happening, certain dragons shape the fog. A rabbit. A fish. Something that might be a boat or might be a very fat bird. The sculptures hold for a few minutes, then the breeze takes them apart and the dragon starts again. They don't seem to mind the impermanence. If anything, it's the point. What is it *for*? Nothing, perhaps; and perhaps that is the whole of the answer. Manami and I have taken to watching them together from the ridge, two women with sore knees and no particular schedule. She brings two bowls of the local brew, the stuff I spent years refusing to call tea. I call it tea now, and mean it; I lost that argument long ago, and am gladder than I expected to have lost it.

Some dragons leave the island. We know this. They slip through the mist and roam the world for years, sometimes centuries. And if they can shape fog here, in this valley, what stops them doing it elsewhere? Every cloud-castle a child has pointed at from a garden. Every horse, every ship, every dragon in the sky. What if none of it was accidental? I have absolutely no evidence for this. It is probably nonsense.
