*29th September 1948*

I have been here long enough now to understand how the island keeps itself hidden. The answer is logistics, of all things, divided among the dragons like chores on a rota.

The fog comes first. A species of dragon the Manaïari call the Fog-Weavers produces it: a deep, rolling mist that rings the entire coast and, on the thick days, buttons the whole valley shut. From the outside, it looks like bad weather. From the inside, it is perfectly clear. I walked to the shore once and stood at the border. On my side, a sunny afternoon. On the other side, a storm that wasn't a storm. The division was sharp enough to put your hand through.

Above the fog, sentinels. Dragons with vision sharp enough to spot a ship at a distance I could not estimate. They watch the ocean the way the harbour master watches the fleet.

If a ship enters the fog, its crew falls asleep. I experienced this myself on the way here and can confirm it is not unpleasant. Nobody has explained the mechanism to my satisfaction, though I have my suspicions about certain small, drowsy-looking dragons I have seen nesting in the mist.

The Ship-Shifters are the largest living things I have seen. I have watched them from the cliffs, and even at a distance the scale is difficult to accept. A single one could wrap its claws around a merchant vessel the way a bird grips a twig. They lift the entire ship and fly it to the far side of the continent. They set it down in open water, pointed in roughly the direction it was already headed. Before the crew wakes, smaller dragons leave kindnesses: a shoal of fish herded into the ship's path, a tailwind arranged, freshwater tanks topped up by a convenient rain. Nothing a sailor would question. Good weather, good luck, good sea. Sailors love to take credit for all three.

During storm season, the Fog-Weavers work hardest. I watched three fly in tight formation through sheets of rain, weaving the fog thicker with every pass. Like someone knitting a wall in a hurricane.

And then there is the Elder. She is always the same dragon. Scales the colour of old jade, eyes that seem to be looking at something behind you. She approaches each sleeping human and blinks once, slowly. On very rare occasions, she nods. When she nods, that human is carried to a village and given a bed. I was profoundly asleep when it happened to me, but I remember the nod. A slight tilt of a vast head, a flicker in those ancient eyes. I have no idea what she saw in me. I rather wish I had been awake to ask, but I suppose that is the point. You do not get to audition.
