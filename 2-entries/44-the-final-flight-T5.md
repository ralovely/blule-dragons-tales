An old dragon went up this morning. The long, unhurried spiral towards the floating islands, no announcement, no looking back; the whole village stopped work to watch until she was a speck, and then everyone went back to what they were doing. I asked Manami what happens afterwards. She took me to a garden.

It was a nest, or had been. A dragon's pile of treasures in a shallow cave, still exactly as its owner left it, except that there is moss on the pebbles now, and small white flowers come up between the trinkets. The island is taking it back, one season at a time.

The Manaïari tend these nest-gardens. Not with grief: they pull the weeds and leave the moss, and they bring their children, who play among the objects and ask what this one was, and that one. "So the memories don't get lonely," Manami said. She was pulling a weed as she said it, the way one does when saying something important and preferring not to make a fuss.
