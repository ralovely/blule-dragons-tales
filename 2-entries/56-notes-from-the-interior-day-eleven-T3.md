*13th October 1955*

Everything here is wet. The floors are wet. My notebooks are wet. I am wet. The lagoon village is built on stilts over a shallow bay, and the entire structure moves with the tide. Through the gaps in the floorboards I can see water, and through the water I can see dragons. I have been gripping the railing since I arrived. The children run between platforms barefoot and laughing. I hate them, gently.

Manami says there is a village further north that moves entirely. Follows a herd of grazing dragons, packs up every few months, relocates. I asked if we could visit. She said "next time," which in Manami's vocabulary sits somewhere between "perhaps" and "absolutely not."

The smallest dragon I have ever seen lives here. It is the size of a cat. It rules the dock. It struts along the railing, claims every post it passes, and produces what it clearly believes is a roar. The sound that comes out would not frighten a moth. The large dragons step around it with exaggerated care, which I first interpreted as respect. Having watched more closely, I now believe it is the same instinct that makes a person step around a very small, very angry dog. Nobody wants to be the one to break the spell.

There is one smaller still. I almost missed it. Eight centimetres, perched between the shoulder blades of a dragon the size of a cart horse. It appeared to be giving directions. The larger dragon appeared to be following them. I watched for ten minutes to be certain I was not hallucinating. I was not. The small one tapped the left shoulder. The large one turned left. I have no explanation for the chain of command at work here, and I am not sure I want one.
