*4th April 1956*

The females of this species write in the sky. Actually write: great swooping sentences traced in flight, visible only in the way the light catches the faint shimmer they leave behind. A banking turn repeated three times means something different from one repeated twice. A sudden climb followed by a stall has a meaning I have not yet decoded. A long, unhurried glide means contentment, I think, or perhaps "the fish are good today." I'm still working out the grammar.

I spent a whole morning on a hilltop with my sketchbook, mapping their flight paths onto paper. Laid flat, the lines looked remarkably like calligraphy: elegant, rhythmic, full of intention. I was rather pleased with myself. I'd filled four pages.

I showed them to Manami that evening. She studied the first page for a long moment, turned it upside down, and handed it back.

"That one says the hunting is good to the east."

I had been reading them backwards. All of them. Four pages of perfectly transcribed ~~nonsense~~ sense, held the wrong way up. Which I suppose is a fair summary of my early years here.
