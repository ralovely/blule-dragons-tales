*26th October 1955*

Every Manaïari I have met wears the same hook of blue stone around their neck. It serves as anchor and compass; ornament seems incidental. A promise made while touching the amulet, I am told, cannot be broken. It is a piece of the island's very heart, worn against their own.

Age does not decide it. A child receives their amulet when the elders (and, it seems, the dragons themselves) deem them *ready*. The rite measures maturity and understanding. To receive one is to be fully seen, to belong to a family and to the land itself. Children make heroic attempts to lose them: down wells, into the sea, once memorably into a pot of soup. They always come back. A lost amulet turns up on a doorstep, or worked into some dragon's pile of trinkets, or back on its hook by morning, and nobody can quite say how. I have my suspicions. They have scales. I wonder if Nangula had one. I never saw her wear it, but then she was good at keeping things close.
