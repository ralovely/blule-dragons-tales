*1st March 1956*

Dragons hoard knick-knackeries. Gold is ignored.

Their caves contain piles of worthless objects. Trinkets. Bric-a-brac, as my mother would say. I have yet to find a single piece of gold among any of them, despite the stuff being everywhere else on this island. The objects amount to a memory hoard.

From a young age, they keep their "treasure" in a seemingly random pile in their cave, yet they know exactly what is where, down to the last pebble. To them, it is the most precious thing in the world: their life story, their identity. You could tell everything about a dragon's personality just by 'reading' its nest. Was it a traveller? a parent? a romantic? a prankster?

In one pile, I noticed a set of false teeth. Gilt wire and porcelain. Its owner knows nothing of dentistry; it just enjoys the clack-clack-clack they make when nudged with a snout.

Another has a single petrified cuckoo's egg. It is convinced this is the pinnacle of avian comedy: a "joke egg" laid by a bird with a magnificent sense of humour.

There is this dragon that collects small black pebbles from the nearby riverbed, adding one every year. A dragon-scale, very slow hourglass.

The most inconvenient must be the entire iron rudder from a small ship. It's enormous. It is, I am quite certain, the dragon equivalent of a child's favourite stuffed bear. The dragon sleeps with one claw hooked fast through the tiller hole, terrified of floating away in its sleep.
