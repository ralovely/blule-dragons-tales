## 2. People

- **Lady Indigo Pepper ("Indy")** — biologist; English father, French mother; bilingual (French = childhood tongue); the only outsider on Aizomea since Nangula; broke her wrist in her second T3 month (30); joined the tooth-brushing line (29); owns an amulet, gifted by an elder woman (27); blue-stained fingertips (05, 09); pockets gold (10); keeps the purple stone (36); windowsill row of gifted objects (12 → 77).
- **Jamie** — narrator of the prologue; River's child, b. 26 Mar 1972; Indigo's only grandchild; never knew their father growing up — "somewhere in these pages" (the 1971 River/Kai arithmetic); found the trunk under the oldest tree via the map in *Le Tour du Monde en 80 Jours*; has since walked a fair portion of Aizomea (53 note); has the compass, the amulet (27 note), the purple stone (36 note), the journals.
- **Cassius Worthington** — husband; scientist of extinct ecosystems; tables and calculations; raised the children during her absences; d. 17 Nov 1954; she never stopped writing to him (31 note; the letter-entry 31 is dated the first anniversary of his death).
- **Nangula Sossusvlei** — Namibian; lifelong solo visitor to Aizomea before Indigo; tall, enormous patience, "moved as though she had been walking for centuries and had decided not to rush"; taught Manami English (04); d. 11 Aug 1935. **She and Indigo never discussed Aizomea** (she kept it secret; died pre-T1) — so all address to her is posthumous. Indigo addresses her directly in 32 ("You spent decades at it before me… never once let slip"; "your compass") and 76 ("How did you know? How will I know?").
- **Cendre** — son, b. 1931; collects rocks (09); pinned her drawings to his wall (P); notebooks, hands-behind-back observer, "his father's son" (65, 67, 68); became an explorer; died a few months after River, in his sleep; the row of stones on his desk (P — two of them from entry 09).

## 3. World mechanics (must survive verbatim in meaning)

- **The compass**: sphere the size of a tangerine, carved dragon scales, blue-tinged metal band etched with stars, opens like a locket; inside a dragon's eye, green-gold, slit pupil; you look *through* it and one slice of horizon is brighter/more vivid — turn until the colours sing (P, 19). It does not point north; "It was looking" — likely the blue stone's call (32). It was **Nangula's**, passed to Indigo with the guardianship ("your compass", 32; 76), and ends in the trunk for Jamie (P). A carried thing: each keeper waits to be sure of the next (76).
- **The blue stone**: hums (eggs hum **in pairs**); source = caves inside the floating islands; harvest is the **only accepted reason a human ever rides a dragon** — all-women riders who sing on the ascent (09). Harvesters' fingertips/palms stain permanently blue (05, 09). Mountains are blue at the peaks — stained memory of stone that has risen (09). Uses: pendants/amulets, thimbles, cooking pots, tools, mortar of glowing walls, poultice (12, 30), toothpaste (29), pigment (12), ice cream (63), gourd-voices (06). Glows at night, brighter at full moon (70); nesting dragons' teeth glow as a perimeter fence (29). **Framing (author): a valuable, non-rare but hard-won resource — everyday uses beside near-extraordinary properties; respect, not reverence; never the word "magic."**
- **The amulet**: blue-stone hook worn by every Manaïari; a promise made touching it cannot be broken; conferred when *ready*, not at an age (25); forged white-hot by fire dragon then cracked-cold by ice dragon — most stones shatter (26); all glow during the humming season (27; Indigo's still glows annually in the trunk, 27 note). **Very nearly impossible to lose** — a lost one always reappears (doorstep, a dragon's trinket pile, back on its hook); the dragons are the unstated agents ("I have my suspicions. They have scales.", 25); ties to the dragons-return-things motif (button in 21, overnight gifts in 12).
- **The humming season**: dragons curl and hum; a single chord re-tunes the world and keeps the floating islands afloat; offerings of spiced honeyed milk; days of stillness (27). Annual, "around the same date" (~8 Nov). Triggers the fluffy species' allergy = Runny Nose Season (28).
- **Island concealment**: Fog-Weavers ring the coast (thin in the south, deliberately — 20); sentinel watchers above; fog puts crews to sleep; Ship-Shifters carry vessels past, set down on course; small kindnesses (fish, tailwind, rain in the tanks); the Elder blinks/nods; the nodded-at are carried to a village (18, 19).
- **Arrival rule of thumb**: "You do not get to audition" (18). Some Manaïari leave the island ("quiet returns", 14; the young man at the water's edge, 33; "a few leave; most come back").
- **The south**: purist dragons; the Tangle (wasps, sound-grasping vines, hiccup meadow); not hidden — *left alone*; a handful of humans reached it over the centuries; "None of them left. The tales do not say what happened to them. Nobody asks." (20). **Never resolve the mystery.** Its teeth, always off-page: the scarred veteran and the singed knot (22); the empty boat returned to the sea, its sailor's fate a one-line inference ("a very large animal with an appetite", 33).
- **The gift economy**: no money, no ownership-word, gold worthless ("It's soft. It bends. What would you do with it?"), favours remembered not tracked (04, 10). The merchant-vocabulary tension (fix 10) resolved via the ~~merchants~~ strikethrough in 17 — her pen reaches for market words; the island has none.
- **Listeners**: warm palms, mostly women, ~30 in the village; knowledge arrives wordlessly, "like hearing colour"; 14 words for dragon silence, 7 learnable (05). Listeners translate dragon stories at the Knotted Cord (21).
- **Sound instruments**: seashell (carries over water), clicked stones (through rock), feather-string hum (shy species), gourds incl. blue-dust voice; response is movement, not sound; "It's not the note. It's the breath behind it." (06).
- **Language**: water-over-stones sound; formal register near dragons; emotions/weather are visitors; no "own/possess"; the tense for things whose effects persist; dragon-sound loanwords; no written form (Indigo first to transcribe); place names revise themselves; paths warm with use; blue ≈ home (04).
- **Dragons**: do not speak, ever (05). Body language: bared teeth = hello; tail grammar; circling = something important (incl. naps); one leg = dissent-without-argument (59). Boredom stages: tail, rearrangement, stare, grin; cure is a job (60). Memory: epochs yes, breakfast no — hence the Knotted Cord exchange: mayflies for mountains (21). Hoards = knick-knack autobiographies, never gold (43). Deaths: fade / swim out / fly to a floating island / slow petrification (the hills have faces); some "become the island" (44). The final flight witnessed once, T5 (45); nest-gardens tended after death (45). Mouelleubleus: blue depth = age; dust-cheats fool no one (46). Dream-fishing (47). Sky-calligraphy by females; Indigo read it upside down (51). Lantern Keepers (52). The collective sigh, unexplained (31). Mimics (61). "The cold-ache" = her coinage for brain freeze (63). Eggs: hundreds of kinds; floaters on strings; most never hatch → stone, furniture; doorstops sometimes hatch years later (34, 35). Hatchlings drink goat milk and dragon-milk sap (36). Kintsugi shells = birth records (37). Dragons *can* fight — the old quarrel, long ago, recorded only in scars and one fused knot (22): they remain, after all, very large animals with appetites (33).
- **The riding rule** (09) vs **being carried** (18): distinct acts — riding is harvest-only; carrying is the dragons' own act (Ship-Shifters, the rite of arrival). **Fix 14: no change, author decision.**
- **Asleep-yet-remembers-the-nod** (18): deliberate paradox, island magic. **Fix 11: no change; preserve paradox.**
- **Tea**: grievances in 03, 17, 35 → full surrender in 75 ("I call it tea now, and mean it"), with the wondering whether it could travel home.
