## A · Blue fingers: preserve the reader's discovery, acknowledge Pepper's knowledge

**Evidence.** [05](../../manuscript/2-entries/05-whispers-of-the-heart-T1.md), 20 July 1938, final paragraph:

> The faint blue has deepened since I arrived; it does not wash off, and I cannot account for it. What, on this island, is dyeing me?

[09](../../manuscript/2-entries/09-the-blue-stones-kiss-T1.md), 29 June 1938, paragraph beginning “One of the harvesters”:

> One of the harvesters let me hold a raw stone today. […] When I gave it back, my fingertips were blue. […] All the harvesters have them, she said. […] It does not wash off.

The entries are 21 days apart in the opposite order to their presentation. Pepper knows the source by 29 June. She might still wonder about the physical mechanism or further darkening, but “What […] is dyeing me?” asks for the source, not its mechanism. Reading it as merely scientific curiosity does not adequately reconcile the wording.

**Provenance.** The historical entry plan deliberately ends 05 on unexplained fingers and preserves the 09 reveal. The [July dating table](https://github.com/ralovely/blule-dragons-tales/commit/0ab2442e14d333771421cec4cab1d113f10e0a4a) then places 09 before 05; [dateline application](https://github.com/ralovely/blule-dragons-tales/commit/1c83ebc) added the dates. September author review retained this arrangement; October's Sayo changes did not touch the ending. The conflict cannot be blamed on naming Sayo.

**Recommendation A:** make the question a recollection without naming blue stone ahead of 09. This is a knowledge-state clarification, not a new stone property. The tradeoff is that 05 no longer claims the mystery is unresolved at composition time; it remains unresolved for the reader. The proposed wording retains the stain's deepening and permanence.

**Alternative:** leave 05's present uncertainty and redate either 05 or 09. This preserves the strongest immediate mystery but changes the approved chronology and needs a separate date decision, including the 05→06 relationship and 05's “first months” cue. No replacement date is proposed here. Adding an unobserved exposure or a second kind of stain would invent facts and is not recommended. Protect Sayo's author-edited parenthesis, the silent dragons, the harvest-only riding rule and Cendre's two samples.

## B · Hana: oral storytelling needs no new literacy history

**Evidence.** [04](../../manuscript/2-entries/04-language-T1.md), 5 April 1938:

> There is no written form. Everything is oral. I may be the first person to write Manaïari words down […] in a spelling system I am inventing as I go.

[73](../../manuscript/2-entries/73-bedtime-stories-T5.md), 26 May 1971:

> One dragon arrives every evening at Hana's window as she reads to her children. […] at exactly the angle of a child being read to.

This is **not a deductive contradiction**: a person in an oral-language community could read another language, and 33 years separate the accounts. But neither Hana's literacy, a book, nor an adopted script is established. Those possibilities explain what could happen, not what the author has chosen. The ledger continues to protect an unwritten language.

**Provenance.** [The resident edit](https://github.com/ralovely/blule-dragons-tales/commit/793bc06345aa10065f9ffe18b1285601f8c38588) added the literal “as she reads” phrase; the preceding text already had the reading simile. [The resident thread](https://ampcode.com/threads/T-01a10876-3693-762c-a981-3ba7801e3947) records approval to make the mother Hana and subsequent shipping, but no separate literacy decision. Its author-approved proposal did not specify literal reading. Approval to ship is not evidence of a discussed script or book.

**Recommendation B:** use “tells her children stories” only in the literal action. Indigo can compare the dragon with an English child being read to; that simile asserts no island literacy. This preserves Hana's motherhood, Manami's implicit grandmotherhood, Ren's separate household, the dragon waiting all night, and the bilingual lullaby ending.

**Alternative:** retain literal reading only if the author intends an exception and chooses what it means. Do not invent imported books, English tuition for Hana or a later writing system as a repair. If B is selected, update the living ledger's “mother reading” description; do not rewrite the historical resident log as though its original implementation never happened.

## C · Boredom: six weeks is enough for the incidents, not “months” of this sequence

**Evidence.** [60](../../manuscript/2-entries/60-when-a-dragon-is-bored-T2-T5.md), 14 July 1948:

> A week later, another dragon spent a morning arranging fish bones […] It has been doing this for months.

T2 begins 2 June: 42 elapsed days before this dateline. If the laundry incident and the next dragon's start both occur during this stay, the latter activity has at most about five weeks. The other Tuesday incident can fit; its Tuesday is unspecified. [59](../../manuscript/2-entries/59-dragon-body-language-T1-T2.md), 4 August, already refers to the boredom notes.

**Classification and provenance.** The “months” phrase and T2–T5 tag predate the datelines; the original manuscript contains the same claim. The dating table chooses T2 because of the letter to the children and the dependency in 59. The tag permits mixed provenance but does not identify this sentence as a 1971 addition. “Considerable fieldwork” could include earlier visits; the present perfect and “A week later” still make the current sequence read as continuous.

**Recommendation C:** “weeks” is the smallest repair under the current-stay reading. This narrows duration, so approve it explicitly. No boredom stage, incident, job, joke or later cross-reference changes. If “months” is intentional, the author should instead identify this as remembered or reported material, or select a later addition; none is established by the evidence. Moving the date risks 59 and is unnecessary for the recommended wording. Do not remove the dual-trip filename merely to simplify this passage.

## D · Market: no collision established

**Evidence.** [62](../../manuscript/2-entries/62-bartering-for-baubles-T4.md), 19 June 1963:

> I took Cendre and River there this morning. […] The button, meanwhile, has already been placed at the centre of the stall's display. […] I walked past this morning […] River had wandered off by then.

She can exchange the button, leave, pass again and find River at the docks before noon. “Already” suits prompt rearrangement of the display. No sentence requires an intervening day. [63](../../manuscript/2-entries/63-the-grand-gourmands-T4.md), 26 June, places River at the docks “every morning this week”; a week has elapsed since 62.

**Recommendation D:** retain. The optional “again before lunch” makes the second pass explicit without asserting another date, but is not required for consistency. Do not redate 62, change the fourth-word flirtation, name the still-unnamed fisherman in this passage, or turn the pebble's value into an R1 economy explanation.

## E · Hammocks: resolve the time of writing

**Evidence.** [64](../../manuscript/2-entries/64-mirrors-to-humanity-T4.md), 10 July 1963:

> Yesterday, a man fell asleep […] and this morning, the same dragon had wedged itself […] By the end of the week, three more dragons had done the same.

10 July 1963 is Wednesday. A finished weekly spread cannot follow that morning's first imitation within the same dated account. An unmarked addition can explain the text's composition, but the page does not identify one. Unlike 62, mere reordering within the morning does not solve the normal reading. The same temporal wording appears in the initial manuscript and survived the early editing pass; this is older than the applied date.

**Recommendation E:** “Last week” / “the next morning” gives one retrospective account from 10 July. It changes the relative placement of these existing events, not the entry date or their week-long development. This is why approval is required. It leaves River watching the whole sequence and Manami's silent recognition intact.

**Alternative:** preserve “Yesterday” and “this morning”, then visibly mark the subsequent update, for example `P.S. By the end of the week…`. That keeps the events relative to 10 July but adds journal furniture and moves the emotional payoff into an addendum. Do not quietly change “week” to “day”: that compresses the established spread and still needs a time-of-writing adjustment.

## F · Hiccupper: meeting interval is not island absence

**Evidence.** [53](../../manuscript/2-entries/53-notes-from-the-interior-day-one-T3.md) meets the Hiccupper on 3 October 1955. T3 departure is approximately 30 April 1956. T4 arrival is 5 June 1963. [65](../../manuscript/2-entries/65-i-brought-them-here-T4.md), 7 August 1963:

> I have been gone eight years. […] It has, in the eight years since I last saw it, grown considerably […]

The island absence is approximately **seven years and one month**, ending at T4 arrival, not at the August outing. The two dated Hiccupper scenes are **seven years, ten months and four days** apart. The second “eight years” is ordinary rounding; the first is a poor description of time away from the island. It could instead mean absence from this settlement, in which case the rounded figure is plausible. The current page leaves that referent unresolved.

**Recommendation F:** explicitly distinguish the two intervals with “I was away for over seven years” and “nearly eight years since I last saw it”. The latter is a precision clarification, not proof that the existing round number is wrong. An even smaller alternative is to change only the first sentence to `I last saw it nearly eight years ago.` and leave the rounded repetition; this loses the island-absence statement and repeats the next paragraph's information.

The [September author review](https://github.com/ralovely/blule-dragons-tales/commit/5c34892899c4e448c63dd62a01503e09f798d3f2) specifically restored “I do not know how it knew me. By smell, perhaps. By something else, perhaps.” Leave that passage intact. Preserve the 7 August/River's thirtieth-birthday alignment without adding a birthday celebration, and preserve the measuring P.S. and Cassius reference. No departure, meeting or birthday is moved.

## G · Surfing: expose the approved split without dating an invented day

**Evidence.** [66](../../manuscript/2-entries/66-the-sea-the-surfers-T3-T5.md) opens `18th April 1956`. Sumi surfs and directs the fleet in this body. Its final paragraph begins:

> River, on her second visit, took to it immediately. […] Kai takes her out at dawn.

River's first visit is 1963; her second is 1971. The tag is T3–T5, but the single visible dateline covers the whole page for a reader unaware of the filename. This is a **presentation defect around settled chronology**, not a request to decide which trip River takes.

**Provenance matters.** [The June decision](https://github.com/ralovely/blule-dragons-tales/commit/f4d0930d460cfce9919f3fb293d24ed0c273e2a3) approved changing the old entry 65's T3–T4 tag to T3–T5 instead of adding a proposed later addendum. This preceded universal datelines. The reconciled R1 brief now explicitly reopens visible presentation. Adding a marker is therefore a fresh proposal, not an overdue implementation of that old decision. The October resident pass assigned Sumi to the 1956 body and preserved the River/Kai paragraph exactly.

**Recommendation G:** `*P.S. 1971.*` before the final paragraph. It states only the settled year, follows the approved P.S. convention, and does not add an eighth Jamie editor's note. Cost: it presents the final paragraph as Pepper's later addition. If the author prefers Jamie's thematic assembly to a journal postscript, use a standalone `*1971*` sub-dateline instead; the evidence does not establish the physical archive format.

Do not redateline the whole entry to 1971, alter Sumi's period, remove “second visit”, or explain Jamie's parentage. A retrospective rewrite of the entire surfing body would be a larger, unnecessary intervention into author-reviewed prose.

## H · Chestnut: withdraw precision rather than manufacture a day

**Evidence.** The [prologue](../../manuscript/1-prologue.md) says “She was six”, “A six-year-old”, and later “named by a six-year-old girl with a spare button”. The ledger gives Indigo's birth as **9 June 1901** and Chestnut's naming as **30 April 1907**. Indigo is five on that April date. Being six places the event somewhere from 9 June 1907 through 8 June 1908; it does not establish one particular day.

**Provenance.** [The birthday-table commit](https://github.com/ralovely/blule-dragons-tales/commit/821e606bc0b7efbb734433de66c74a2308b1dbfa) introduced the precise naming day as a proposed private anniversary. The living ledger later adopted the dates; the reconciled references now explicitly expose the conflict. The day is not in the prologue. Removing it still amends canon and requires approval.

**Recommendation H:** replace the precise naming-day claim in both ledger §1 and §2b with age-six wording and “exact date unspecified”; retain an approximate period only as approximate. Update the chronology's conflict register and timeline once approved. This does not choose a new day. It preserves the button origin, Chestnut's near-century return, and Indigo's 9 June birthday alignments in 12 and 74's year.

**Alternative:** retain 30 April but choose a different year, or retain 1907 and choose a day after her birthday. Either invents a new precise event date without manuscript evidence, so neither is recommended. Changing Indigo's birthday or making her five damages more established material than removing this reference-only precision.
