# Notes for design

An expandable notebook of illustrated-book possibilities. Add to it as the project develops; these are exploratory ideas, not approved layouts, manuscript edits or new canon. Record any later author decisions separately from the possibilities they resolve.

## 29 October 2026 — Drawing much more inspiration from Dautremer's books

Creative context: the author wants a LOT of inspiration from Dautremer's books as complete works: text, illustrations, humour, atmosphere, composition and their interplay. See the [tone references](../../reference/tone-research/README.md).

The central possibility: make this feel less like **a manuscript that will eventually be illustrated** and more like **an illustrated object whose words, pictures and margins tell the story together**. Not simply more whimsy in Pepper's sentences, but more invention across the whole book.

### 1. Let illustrations quietly disagree with Pepper

Pepper records one thing; the reader can see something she has missed. This gives her a companion voice without adding another narrator.

In the Gerald material, she insists that he never moves. Small drawings across a spread could show him in apparently identical poses, but an object has changed position each time. Nothing proves he is responsible. Her observational confidence becomes part of the joke.

### 2. Give ordinary things extravagantly serious attention

A boot, a sleeping position or an unsuitable chair can deserve the visual treatment normally reserved for a magnificent dragon.

Pepper's retained English boots could have a meticulous study: repairs, scorch marks, mud she cannot remove, an unsuccessful local improvement. The dragon responsible for the damage might occupy only a small corner.

The disproportion is the humour: enormous creatures, tiny domestic consequences, immense scholarly care. Any depicted incident or local improvement remains a proposal, not an established event.

### 3. Make the page carry evidence, not just decoration

Her notebooks could visibly accumulate attempts to understand:

- A dragon sketch repeatedly extended beyond its original boundary.
- Several unsuccessful diagrams of a tail gesture.
- A colour swatch beside a note admitting that the colour is wrong.
- A measurement abandoned because the subject sat on the equipment.

These additions should reveal an encounter or Pepper's temperament, not merely make the page look scrapbook-like. Proposed captions and incidents still need the same editorial judgment as other new content.

### 4. Turn some subjects into visual inventories

Existing candidates include hoards, eggs, sleeping arrangements and gifts.

A hoard spread could present seemingly worthless objects with the dignity of museum specimens. Pepper supplies precise descriptions; the arrangement suggests attachments she cannot explain.

The windowsill gifts offer a recurring device: **the same view of the sill, gradually filling across the book**. By “I packed them all,” the reader has spent time with those objects too. Preserve established gifts and payoffs when developing the inventory.

### 5. Let abundance and emptiness have different emotional jobs

Some spreads could be crowded, tangential, almost impossible to finish exploring. Others should offer almost nothing.

For entry 74 (dinner, shoulders touching, excellent food), the illustration need not explain the romance. A long table, plentiful activity, River and Kai somewhere within it. Pepper's tiny paragraph leaves the reader to find them. Preserve the relationship's implication rather than making it explicit.

For the Cassius entry (31), the opposite: room around the final address. No visual explanation of grief.

### 6. Allow beauty to remain peculiar

Dragons need not be consistently majestic or consistently cute. An exquisite creature can have an awkward resting posture. A splendid composition can contain a practical embarrassment. Tenderness can coexist with ungainliness.

This could keep the book affectionate without making Aizomea polished or picturesque.

### Possible starting studies

The suggested first studies are the **Gerald spread, the hoards spread and the final windowsill**. Together they would test visual comedy, invented scholarship and accumulated emotion without rewriting the book into a different voice. This is a recommendation to explore, not an approved production sequence.
