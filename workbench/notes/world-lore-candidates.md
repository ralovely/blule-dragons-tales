# world-lore-candidates.md — major world facts (brainstorm, PENDING author review)

For the graphical timeline near the prologue, and for the later canon-enrichment exercise. **None of this is canon yet.**

**Brief (author, 2026-07-04):** events at DPA scale (structural, not anecdotal), within Pepper/River/Jamie's lifetimes (~1890s–1990s); sober register — the goal is to anchor the story in reality. World premise: dragons are well known the way whales or snow leopards are known — real, documented, *rare* — a deliberate liberty from the Wyrmspan facts' more omnipresent-dragon tone. Wyrmspan facts (writing/agent-resources/wyrmspan_dragon_facts.csv) are canon but not actively reused; key corpus givens honoured here: dracologist guilds, amateur dragon-watchers, the pre-1972 venom trade, the DPA's clauses (venom ban, working-conditions, harassment sanctions), a wyvern named posthumously for Nangula Sossusvlei.

The arc: bounties → extinctions → institutions → science → law — the real 20th-century conservation story with dragons in it. Real-world date rhymes marked ®.

1. **End of the bounty era (1889–1904)** — European states repeal standing dragon bounties; the ledgers become the primary evidence of how much was lost.
2. **The European extinction (1911)** — the Alpine Greatwing, the continent's last large native species, declared extinct. Dracology's Great Auk: the founding shame the modern discipline organises itself around.
3. **"The last dragon in Britain" (1926)** — officially recorded gone after a decade without a verified sighting. *(Dramatic irony: Lady Chestnut is asleep behind an English estate the whole time.)*
4. **First university chair of dracology (Uppsala 1919; Oxford 1937)** — before that, the field belonged to the guilds and amateurs.
5. **The Guild Consolidation (1931)** — regional hunters' guilds and scholarly societies merge into the modern Dracologists' Guild (classification, licensing, field ethics). Gives the corpus's "guilds" their history.
6. **The Silent Skies of 1934** — the Atlantic flyway migration fails to appear for one season. No cause ever established; the most studied non-event in the field.
7. **The last of the Hans dynasty (1937)** — the great hunting house ends with the sixth Hans; the trophy hall bequeathed, unwanted, to the nation. *(Canon tie: entry 32.)*
8. **The wartime doctrine (1940)** — early military-use attempts fail badly on both sides; belligerents quietly adopt identical leave-alone policies. One of the war's only universally honoured conventions.
9. **The Chicago corridors (1944 ®)** — the convention founding modern civil aviation charts known migration corridors as permanent air-exclusion zones ("the old lanes").
10. **The first world survey (1948 ®, IUCN founding year)** — census concludes fewer than two thousand large dragons survive in the wild outside unmapped ranges. THE scarcity-establishing fact.
11. **R. v. Halloran (1949)** — landmark case: killing a dragon is not "pest control" but requires a licence; ends casual shooting in the Commonwealth two decades before the DPA.
12. **The synthetic venom collapse (1951)** — a laboratory substitute renders the venom trade uneconomic; the Marseille warehouses close within five years. Conservation by chemistry, not conscience.
13. **The industrial-use ban (1953)** — post-war proposals to employ fire-breathing species in power generation rejected by international agreement after a single pilot programme. **⚠ Deliberate liberty with the Wyrmspan Steely Fae fact:** industrial use reframed as a rejected proposal/pilot rather than widespread practice, to keep dragons non-omnipresent; the DPA working-conditions clause reads as its echo. Author to confirm.
14. **The Karakoram discovery (1954)** — a remnant breeding population found at altitude; first confirmed wild breeding site known to science in the century.
15. **Scale ring-dating (1955)** — shed-scale analysis proves lifespans exceeding five hundred years, overturning the assumed 60–80-year span and rewriting every population model.
16. **First Red Data Book listing (1966 ®)** — all remaining dragon species at the highest threat category.
17. **The last dragon-hunting ship decommissioned (1963)** — the Norwegian *Sigrid* returns to Sandefjord empty for the fourth consecutive season and does not sail again.
18. **The UN special session (1970)** — first General Assembly debate on draconic conservation; two years of drafting follow. *(The occasion of the prologue's photograph: Indigo outside the UN in the hat.)*
19. **The Dragon Protection Act (1972)** — CANON ANCHOR. Venom and derivative trade outlawed; licensing abolished in favour of full protection; working-use and deliberate-harassment clauses. (Clauses per the Wyrmspan corpus.)
20. **CITES Appendix I (1973 ®)** — dragon-derived goods listed in the treaty's founding year, closing the international loophole the DPA left open.

## Why this shape serves the story
Explains why Indigo's world laughed at living-dragon research (they were nearly gone), why Aizomea matters (the reservoir — the place the world's numbers don't count), and what her public life's work was swimming against. Three quiet birth-year rhymes: 1901 (bounties ending as Indigo is born), 1933 (River born the year before the Silent Skies), 1972 (Jamie and the Act).
