*4th September 1963*

There is no snow on Aizomea, but that has not stopped the ice dragons from inventing snowball fights. They make their own, breathing frost onto the ground until a patch of mountainside is covered in a thick, crunchy white that has no business being in the middle of the Pacific. Then they pack it with their claws, test the weight, and stockpile ammunition behind ridges like small, scaled generals preparing for war. The fights are joyful, more pillow fight than battle, and they can last all afternoon. The Manaïari children join in, woefully outmatched but fearless. River was in the thick of it within minutes. Cendre watched from what he believed was a safe distance. I watched from what I believed was a safer one. We were both wrong by about forty feet. The snowball that hit me was the size of a watermelon. The one that hit Cendre was larger. He took it with dignity. River, already covered in snow, cheered.

The migratory birds have learnt to avoid the area during play hours. The ones that haven't are pelted mid-flight, tumbling from the sky in a puff of feathers and indignation. The dragons catch them before they hit the ground. It seems motivated by hunger more than cruelty. It is a game that doubles as lunch. Some would find it morally complicated. I might too, if I were not eating a chicken leg as I'm writing this.
